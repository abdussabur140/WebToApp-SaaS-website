Without starting over

A mobile app shouldn't mean rebuilding your website.

Many businesses already have a website that works on phones. Rebuilding that work as a separate native app takes time and creates a second product to maintain. WebToApp uses WebView technology to bring your existing site into an app, so the content people see still comes from your website.

  • Reuse what you have built

    Keep the pages, content, and layouts you already maintain, instead of recreating them natively.

  • Keep the brand consistent

    Plan an app icon, splash screen, and navigation that match the website your customers already know.

Responsive websites

Your website, ready for phone screens.

A WebView app shows your site at phone width. The best results come from a responsive site that already works on small screens.

  • Readable text and comfortable tap targets

    Buttons and links should be easy to tap without zooming.

  • Navigation that works with touch

    Menus should open, close, and move between pages without relying on hover.

  • Forms that suit mobile keyboards

    Test sign-in and other forms on a phone-sized screen before you plan the app.

Use cases

Who WebToApp is for.

Different teams use a website-to-app approach for different reasons. Here is how it could fit each one.

  • SaaS founders

    Add a mobile entry point to a web product you already maintain. App navigation and branding are planned around your product, and content still comes from your website.

  • E-commerce businesses

    Bring your store into a branded app with navigation to categories, products, and account pages. Checkout behaviour in a WebView depends on your site and store policies, so test it before launch.

  • Agencies and freelancers

    Deliver a mobile app alongside a client's website, with its own branding. Confirm each client's platform, integration, and publishing responsibilities at the start.

  • Content publishers

    Give readers an app experience for articles, video, or newsletters on your existing site. Push notifications and deep links need configuration and platform setup.

  • Existing web application owners

    Wrap a responsive web application for mobile users. Test sign-in, device features, and offline behaviour on each target platform before you release.

FAQ

Questions before you start.

Feature availability depends on your website and project. Confirm the details that matter to you before relying on a specific integration.

How does website-to-app conversion work?

WebToApp uses WebView technology to present your existing website inside an Android or iOS app. Content comes from your website, and app navigation, branding, and supported integrations are configured for the app.

Do I need to rebuild my website?

No. The approach is built around your existing website. Check that it works well at phone width, because the app shows it there.

Does WebToApp support Android and iOS?

Both are target platforms. Each has its own requirements, permissions, and store rules, so confirm availability for your project and plan.

Can I customize my app's branding?

You can plan the app name, icon, splash screen, and theme. The options available depend on the platforms and features your project includes.

Do all websites support every native integration?

No. Features such as push notifications, camera access, location, and deep links depend on your website, app configuration, platform support, and permissions.

What is required for app store publishing?

You need developer accounts with Apple and Google, app signing, and store listing details. Store review policies apply, including rules for WebView-based apps.

Confirm your plan's build and publishing scope before release.
